# Literature Review: Using NLP and Explainable ML to Identify Privacy Harms

> Practicum artifact (Dec 2025), extracted from the privacy-heuristics repo 2026-07-21.

# 1\. Introduction and Problem Statement

Existing privacy frameworks, while conceptually influential, often lack empirical validation against real-world user reactions and documented harms. Foundational theories such as Solove’s taxonomy of privacy (2006) and Nissenbaum’s theory of contextual integrity (2004) provide essential language for discussing privacy, but offer limited predictive value for practitioners making early-stage product decisions. This gap forces reliance on ad-hoc checklists or compliance frameworks that are not grounded in the lived experiences of users or the patterns of documented incidents.

This review synthesizes recent research (2018–2025) that bridges this gap by applying natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning (ML) to real-world data sources. The goal is to survey the current state-of-the-art in empirically identifying privacy harms and to highlight the emerging trend towards explainable, actionable guidance. This meta-study reveals a clear trajectory from descriptive analysis to a nascent push for predictive tools, thereby establishing the critical research gap this proposal aims to fill.

# 2\. Thematic Synthesis of Current Research

The literature converges around three key themes: (1) methodologies for eliciting and analyzing user-perceived privacy harms from text; (2) the large-scale, automated analysis of privacy artifacts; and (3) the development of explainable outputs to make findings actionable for practitioners.

**Theme 1: Eliciting and Analyzing User-Perceived Harms**

A significant body of work validates the use of user-generated text to uncover privacy concerns that experts often miss. Jin et al. (2021) introduced the "Lean Privacy Review," demonstrating that crowdworkers could efficiently identify salient privacy issues early in the design process. This user-centric approach is complemented by large-scale analyses of unsolicited user feedback. Studies by Zhang et al. (2025), Ebrahimi et al. (2022), Yang & Hasan (2024), and Al-Rubaie et al. (2022) all develop NLP pipelines to mine privacy harms from mobile app reviews. Collectively, these papers establish that user-generated data is a rich, indispensable source for grounding privacy analysis in empirical reality.

**Theme 2: Large-Scale Automated Analysis of Privacy Artifacts**

Parallel to the focus on user feedback, researchers have developed sophisticated tools for analyzing formal privacy documents and incident reports at scale. Harkous et al. (2018) pioneered this with *Polisis*, a deep-learning framework for making privacy policies queryable and understandable. Building on this, Story et al. (2019) created *MAPS*, a system that correlates policy text with app behavior to flag compliance discrepancies. More recently, Qin et al. (2025) developed tools to interpret personal data types in incident reports, assisting with GDPR-compliant breach notifications. This research demonstrates the feasibility of moving beyond manual analysis to automated systems that can reveal systemic risks.

**Theme 3: Moving Towards Explainable and Actionable Outputs**

A crucial trend across this body of work is the emphasis on explainability. The goal is not merely to identify harms but to present them in a way that practitioners can understand and act upon. The topic models from Zhang et al. (2025) produce human-interpretable themes. The user-authored risk assessments from Huang et al. (2023) map directly to mitigation strategies. The taxonomies generated by Qin et al. (2025) are designed specifically to support privacy professionals. This focus on interpretability reflects a growing demand for practical tools in privacy engineering.

# 3\. Annotated Lit Review

[**Lean Privacy Review: Collecting Users’ Privacy Concerns of Data Practices at a Low Cost**](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3463910)

* **Date:** 2021 (ACM TOCHI)  
* **Summary:** Introduces a low-cost method (Lean Privacy Review, LPR) to elicit users’ privacy concerns about proposed data practices early in design via crowdworkers. LPR decomposes a data practice into components, gathers targeted user feedback, and compiles concerns for practitioners. The study reports that LPR recovers most issues found by experts while also surfacing additional, user-salient concerns.  
* **Relation to proposal:** Demonstrates that user feedback can efficiently surface privacy harms missed by experts. Validates the value of integrating user-perceived harms into design-time checks.

[**Mining User Privacy Concern Topics from App Reviews**](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0164121225000238)

* **Date:** 2025 (Information & Software Technology); arXiv 2022  
* **Summary:** Presents an interpretable NLP pipeline to identify privacy-relevant reviews and discover privacy concern topics in mobile app reviews. A classifier filters privacy-related comments, and a topic modeling stage yields coherent, human-interpretable concern themes that outperform LDA baselines.  
* **Relation to proposal:** Directly aligns with mining user-generated text for privacy harms. The interpretable topic outputs can seed explainable heuristics.

[**Unsupervised Summarization of Privacy Concerns in Mobile App Reviews**](https://fahimehebrahimi.github.io/files/ASE.pdf)

* **Date:** 2022 (ASE)  
* **Summary:** Develops an unsupervised approach for summarizing privacy concerns from large corpora of mobile app reviews. The method addresses sparsity and noise, producing concise summaries that capture prevalent privacy issues without extensive manual labeling.  
* **Relation to proposal:** Supports scalable extraction of user-expressed privacy harms from noisy, real-world text—useful as an ingestion/summarization layer for a multi-source pipeline.

[**Discovering Privacy Harms from Education Technology by Analyzing User Reviews**](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3689943.3695050)

* **Date:** 2024 (ACM WPES)  
* **Summary:** Conducts a thematic analysis of thousands of EdTech app reviews and uncovers recurring privacy and safety concerns (e.g., excessive tracking, intrusive monitoring). The work structures user-perceived harms into themes and sub-themes.  
* **Relation to proposal:** Empirical evidence that unsolicited user reviews reveal concrete, domain-specific privacy harms.

[**A Review-based Approach to Generate Privacy Control Rules Using Machine Learning and** NLP](https://www.google.com/search?q=https://doi.org/10.1109/BigData55660.2022.9833729)

* Date: 2022 (IEEE Big Data)

* Summary: Develops a system that uses NLP and ML to analyze user reviews from the Google Play store to automatically generate specific, actionable privacy control rules for users.

* Relation to proposal: Directly supports the core idea of translating unstructured user text into structured, actionable outputs (rules/heuristics), providing a strong precedent for the methodology.

[**Mobile Application Privacy Risk Assessments from User-Authored Scenarios**](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~breaux/publications/thuang-re23.pdf)

* Date: 2023 (IEEE RE)

* Summary: Introduces a pipeline where users write usage scenarios from an app screenshot. An NER model extracts personal-data types, and users provide risk scores for those data in context, yielding quantitative risk profiles mapped to mitigation strategies.

* Relation to proposal: Demonstrates a user-in-the-loop, explainable path from perceived risk to actionable design guidance.

[**Automated Expansion of Privacy Data Taxonomy for Compliant Data Breach Notification (GRASP/Tracy)**](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/automated-expansion-of-privacy-data-taxonomy-for-compliant-data-breach-notification/)

* **Date:** 2025 (NDSS)  
* **Summary:** Proposes GRASP, a method for automatically constructing and expanding privacy data taxonomies, and Tracy, an assistant that recognizes and interprets personal data types in incident reports to support GDPR-compliant breach notification.  
* **Relation to proposal:** Illustrates explainable ML over real incident text, organizing outputs into taxonomies that practitioners can act on.

[**Polisis: Automated Analysis and Presentation of Privacy Policies Using Deep Learning**](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity18/sec18-harkous.pdf)

* **Date:** 2018 (USENIX Security)  
* **Summary:** Introduces a deep-learning framework to segment and label privacy policy text at scale, enabling queries, visual summaries, and a QA chatbot (PriBot).  
* **Relation to proposal:** Foundational for policy-text NLP, showing how to build explainable, user-facing artifacts from unstructured legal text.

[**Natural Language Processing for Mobile App Privacy Compliance (MAPS)**](https://usableprivacy.org/static/files/story_pal_2019.pdf)

* **Date:** 2019 (PAL)  
* **Summary:** Describes MAPS, which analyzes both privacy policies and app behavior to flag potential compliance issues at scale by using a classifier for policy text and static analysis to detect discrepancies.  
* **Relation to proposal:** Evidence that large-scale, automated analyses can reveal systematic privacy risks and suggests a model for correlating text-extracted claims with observed practices.
